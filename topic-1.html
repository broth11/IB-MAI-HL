Syllabus content
  • Approximation: decimal places, significant figures.
  • GuidanceStudents should be able to choose an appropriate degree of accuracy based on given data.
  • Upper and lower bounds of rounded numbers.
  • GuidanceIf $x=4.1$ to one decimal place, $4.05 \leq x<4.15$.
  • Percentage errors.
  • GuidanceStudents should be aware of, and able to calculate, measurement errors (such as rounding errors or measurement limitations). For example finding the maximum percentage error in the area of a circle if the radius measured is 2.5 cm to one decimal place.
  • Estimation.
  • GuidanceStudents should be able to recognize whether the results of calculations are reasonable. For example lengths cannot be negative.
Connections
  • Other contextsCurrency approximations are often to nearest whole number, for example peso, yen; to nearest cent, euro, dollar, pound; meteorology, alternative rounding methods.
  • Links to other subjectsOrder of magnitudes (physics); uncertainty and precision of measurement (sciences).
  • Aim 8Caring about approximations; ethical implications.
  • TOKIs mathematical reasoning different from scientific reasoning, or reasoning in other areas of knowledge?

AHL 1.13

Polar and exponential forms of complex numbers

AHLStudy guide
Files
  • Nothing yet
Videos
  • Forms of Complex Numbers (Rectangular, Polar, Euler)
  • Operations of Complex Numbersalso covers this
  • GDC Tips: Complex Numbersalso covers this
Links
  • AHL 1.13 study guide (IB MAI Hub)
Syllabus content
  • Modulus-argument (polar) form: $z=r(\cos \theta+\mathrm{i} \sin \theta)=r \operatorname{cis} \theta .$
  • Exponential form: $z=r \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \theta} .$
  • GuidanceExponential form is sometimes called the Euler form.
  • Conversion between Cartesian, polar and exponential forms, by hand and with technology.
  • Calculate products, quotients and integer powers in polar or exponential forms.
  • GuidanceIn examinations students will not be required to find the roots of complex numbers.
  • Adding sinusoidal functions with the same frequencies but different phase shift angles.
  • GuidancePhase shift and voltage in circuits as complex quantities.Example: Two AC voltages sources are connected in a circuit. If $V_{1}=10 \cos (40 t)$ and $V_{2}=20 \cos (40 t+10)$ find an expression for the total voltage in the form $V=A \cos (40 t+B)$.
  • Geometric interpretation of complex numbers.
  • GuidanceAddition and subtraction of complex numbers can be represented as vector addition and subtraction. Multiplication of complex numbers can be represented as a rotation and a stretch in the Argand diagram.
Connections
  • TOKWhy might it be said that $\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i} \pi}+1=0$ is beautiful? What is the place of beauty and elegance in mathematics? What about the place of creativity?
  • EnrichmentSolution of differential equations by separation of variables (AHL5.15), as both polar and exponential forms are solutions of $\frac{\mathrm{d} y}{\mathrm{~d} \theta}=\mathrm{i} y$.
