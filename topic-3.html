Scalar and vector products

AHLPlaceholder
Placeholder. Lessons and resources for this subtopic are not built yet.

Syllabus content
  • Definition and calculation of the scalar product of two vectors.
  • The angle between two vectors; the acute angle between two lines.
  • GuidanceCalculate the angle between two vectors using $\boldsymbol{v} \cdot \boldsymbol{w}=\|\boldsymbol{v}\| \boldsymbol{w} \mid \cos \theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between two non-zero vectors $\boldsymbol{v}$ and $\boldsymbol{w}$, and ascertain whether the vectors are perpendicular $(\boldsymbol{v} \cdot \boldsymbol{w}=0)$.
  • Definition and calculation of the vector product of two vectors.
  • Guidance$\boldsymbol{v} \times \boldsymbol{w}=\|\boldsymbol{v}\|\|\boldsymbol{w}\| \sin \theta \boldsymbol{n}$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\boldsymbol{v}$ and $\boldsymbol{w}$ and $\boldsymbol{n}$ is the unit normal vector whose direction is given by the righthand screw rule.Not required: generalized properties and proofs of scalar and cross product.
  • Geometric interpretation of $\|\boldsymbol{v} \times \boldsymbol{w}\|$.
  • GuidanceUse of $\|\boldsymbol{v} \times \boldsymbol{w}\|$ to find the area of a parallelogram (and hence a triangle).
  • Components of vectors.
  • GuidanceThe component of vector $\boldsymbol{a}$ acting in the direction of vector $\boldsymbol{b}$ is $\frac{\boldsymbol{a} \cdot \boldsymbol{b}}{\|\boldsymbol{b}\|}=\|\boldsymbol{a}\| \cos \theta$.The component of a vector $\boldsymbol{a}$ acting perpendicular to vector $\boldsymbol{b}$, in the plane formed by the two vectors, is $\frac{\|\boldsymbol{a} \times \boldsymbol{b}\|}{\|\boldsymbol{b}\|}=\|\boldsymbol{a}\| \sin \theta$.
Connections
  • Other contextsComputer graphics: Lighting-normalize one vector onto another to determine the intensity of light on a surface. Perspective-project a three-dimensional vector onto a two-dimensional plane using the scalar product.
  • PhysicsTorque- magnitude of the rotational force ('torque') applied to a point/object is equal to the magnitude of the cross product of the "length" of the lever and the "force" applied to that level. The direction of the torque, for example will the force twist the the nut "on" (tightening) or "off" (loosening) the bolt, is the cross product. Electro-magnetic forces and the right and left hand rules. $\mathrm{W}=\mathrm{F} \cdot \mathrm{d}$. Forces-what component of a vector's force is acting in the direction of another-useful for any "structural" analysis when working out the "strain" on each part of the structure that results from a given force.
  • Links to other subjectsMagnetic forces and fields, and dynamics (physics)
  • TOKWhat counts as understanding in mathematics? Is it more than just getting the right answer?
